# Direct vs Marketplace Strategy

2026-10-13. **CFO recommendation: test single-site annual economics first; defer distribution commitment.** Direct is the preferred learning channel if authorized merchant evidence emerges. Woo becomes preferable only when measurable channel benefits cover its lower receipts and additional obligations. This does not select a paid workflow or authorize a launch.

## Channel comparison

| Decision factor | Direct | Woo Marketplace |
|---|---|---|
| Learning | Can target the exact workflow and inspect onboarding friction | Listing traffic may include poor-fit buyers; traffic is not validation |
| Billing and accounts | Founder chooses eligible payment/billing system; owns customer service, tax/refund operations | Woo handles standard sales/subscriptions and account/refund routing; vendor still owns product support |
| Contribution | Higher retained receipts under the illustrative model | Lower retained receipts and additional review/QIT overhead |
| Acquisition | No built-in distribution; founder sales effort counts in CAC | Potential discovery/trust, but no guaranteed ranking, promotion or sales |
| License packaging | Finite single-site/agency hypotheses are possible, subject to published terms | Current standard standalone offer is single-site; do not assume agency entitlement transfers |
| Release work | Needs security, regression testing, documentation and updates regardless of channel | Adds official review, QIT, coexistence and release procedures |
| Customer data | Direct relationship subject to consent/privacy obligations | Use support data for its authorized purpose; no assumption of an exportable marketing list |
| Operational concentration | Payment vendor dependence plus founder-run sales operations | Marketplace policy, eligibility, payout and listing dependence |
| Exit | Existing subscription and consumer obligations still need funding | Additional removal/support/refund exposure under vendor terms |

Official evidence and policy/ambiguity classification are in [Document 1](01-WOO-MARKETPLACE-POLICY.md). These comparisons are CFO inferences from those rules, not claims that direct sales are operationally free.

## Quantitative channel decision

Use measured channel-specific CAC, refund rate, onboarding, support, retained customers and fixed effort. In the model's base case at USD 149, net receipt difference is **USD 39.634/account/year** (135.59 direct versus 95.956 Woo); extra Marketplace annual fixed cost is **USD 1,460**. Therefore, at equal account counts N, Woo needs acquisition/administrative/support savings or incremental economic value exceeding `39.634×N + 1,460`. At N=100 that is **USD 5,423.40/year**. Do not count gross sales as the benefit.

With identical base costs and a hypothetical established direct cohort of 100 accounts, direct annual contribution is USD 3,484. Woo would need **229 stable billed accounts** to exceed this after its own fixed costs, because `(3,484 + 6,160)/42.206 = 228.50`. This is a comparison threshold, not a traffic, conversion or sales forecast. If Woo replaces direct purchases, those sales are not all incremental. Evaluate channel attribution and cannibalization before claiming a benefit.

For genuinely additional Woo customers while continuing direct sales, charge only incremental shared costs once: `additional Woo accounts × their measured contribution > incremental channel fixed costs + displaced direct contribution`. Do not double-count core maintenance, and do not omit channel administration. Even lower CAC does not remove renewal-time revenue share. No private Marketplace fee/processor schedule or Founder payment eligibility is currently established.

## Pricing structures to evaluate

All amounts: **PRICING HYPOTHESIS — NOT VALIDATED CUSTOMER WTP**. USD annually, before applicable tax. These are alternatives to test, not a pricing decision.

| Option | Candidate | Benefits | Tradeoff and support implications |
|---|---|---|---|
| A: one site | USD 149; challenge with 99 and 199 | Clear payer/store boundary; simplest onboarding and Woo comparison | At 149, small support overruns materially impair economics; test value at an explicit renewal price |
| B: small agency | USD 449 for up to 5 production sites | One accountable operator may reduce acquisition/support duplication | Up to five different environments; require one agency contact and supported scope, no unlimited consultation |
| C: larger agency | USD 999 for up to 25 production sites, only after demand evidence | Larger account value and consolidated administration | Fully activated cost per site is low; correlated incidents and client turnover may overwhelm margin; no presumed fleet/multisite capability |
| D: founding customer | USD 99 for first year, one site, then disclosed USD 149/year | Can reduce initial buyer risk during a bounded learning program | Base first-year contribution is negative; Founder must authorize cohort loss/time cap and clear renewal/refund terms; no lifetime or perpetual discount |

A deserves the first economic test because it has the fewest entitlement/support variables, **not because USD 149 is validated**. B can follow only if a real agency pays for repeated work across several stores. C requires measured full-activation burden and renewal economics. D is optional research expenditure, not a way to manufacture payment evidence through an artificially low perpetual price. A positive founding sale tests only the stated first-year offer; it does not validate the renewal price.

If both channels eventually launch, use identical single-site list pricing until parity interpretation is resolved. Direct agency packages or founding discounts need explicit policy review before public cross-channel use. No lifetime model: no credible service-horizon reserve, support distribution or long-term maintenance cost has been demonstrated. One year's sale must not finance an undefined obligation forever.

## License lifecycle and operational boundary

Test an annual updates/support entitlement with continued installed functionality after expiry, aligned with observed ecosystem practice. Direct entitlement details remain a future Founder decision. Whatever the final entitlement, an expired or unavailable license cannot change mutation authority, strand approved work, hide journals, disable History or make eligible Undo/recovery unsafe. A licensing server must never turn a conflict into an overwrite.

Keep one authoritative Free/core mutation engine. Pro may prepare an additional workflow and invoke frozen Preview → approval → core execution → results; it must not introduce a second writer. Compatible core/add-on versions and safe incompatibility handling need an explicit future contract. No mandatory SaaS without demonstrated economics and customer need. No Pro or licensing implementation is proposed here.

Before sales, the Founder would need to approve actual support hours/coverage, payment eligibility and tax handling, refund language, compatibility envelope and a funded exit plan. Those are launch prerequisites, not actions taken in this research.
