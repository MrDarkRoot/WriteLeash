# Hunter — Database seatbelt: finite authority for a write job

Supplementary research: **2026-10-12 UTC / 2026-10-13 Asia/Ho_Chi_Minh**. Founder-requested expansion beyond conventional pricing features. Baseline main: `325cbe722f7cc0e7d828a3dd620adbc5cd1244c8`. This is independent research, not a roadmap, implementation authorization or replacement commercial decision.

## Finding

**INFERRED:** The more interesting job is giving a useful writer *finite business authority*: a supplier connector, automation or repair operator may make a bounded set of changes, with excessive effects stopped and explained. A Preview describes an intended run; a write contract would constrain its actual effects. That distinction matters only when existing controls fail to constrain an important, recurring job.

**HYPOTHESIS:** A contract could specify objects, fields, permitted transitions, expiry and a cumulative mutation budget. Illustrative instruction: “this approved supplier job may update these 120 SKUs; it may not delete products; pause for approval before more than 5 stocked products become unavailable.” These numbers are examples, not observed customer requirements. Neither task-wide enforcement nor this policy language exists in today's WriteLeash.

No reviewed source establishes willingness to pay for that contract. The strongest operational evidence concerns sync overwrites and unexplained destructive jobs. The most direct fit to original research remains bounded PostgreSQL UPDATE execution; the commercial evidence for it remains weak.

## Red team and narrow next test

**INFERRED:** The strongest incumbent is WP All Import for a certified supplier-job adapter: it owns the job, offers selective handling/hooks, and can incorporate a threshold check closer to execution. Square itself is the more relevant incumbent for Square incidents. An additional plugin could add compatibility responsibility while making legitimate sync less reliable.

The strongest reason to reject the leading workflow is that the reports are mostly source-of-truth/configuration disputes or bugs now addressed by the integration. They do not prove merchants need a separately purchased authority layer. A budget also cannot detect a wrong price on one allowed SKU, and stopping half a job may create another reconciliation problem.

**HYPOTHESIS, proposed artifact test only:** find one existing repeat import with a real anonymized before/after export and execution log. Replay offline to identify affected IDs, repeated writes, transaction/batch boundaries and harmful transitions. Compare one connector-native threshold hook with a putative finite job envelope. Do not build importer, ERP, PIM, SaaS or universal interceptor. Do not modify Free or contact a merchant without Founder authorization.

Founder questions and permission-based requests:

1. “Show the last job that changed more products or states than intended. Which IDs changed, who noticed, and how long did reconciliation actually take?” Ask permission for redacted before/after exports and logs; exclude credentials/customer records.
2. “What remains painful after fixing source-of-truth settings, upgrading the connector, and using its preview/hooks?” Ask for the exact current version/settings, with permission.
3. “Which limit could have stopped that incident without stopping your normal weekly run?” Test their threshold against a normal run and the incident; do not supply a desired answer.
4. “Can this job retry, run concurrently, or write through another API? Who is allowed to grant additional scope?” Request a redacted execution trace, not production access.
5. “Would you buy this separately, ask your integration vendor to include it, or use a small agency script? What did you actually pay last time?” Separate preference from demonstrated purchase and renewal.

**Recommendation: INVESTIGATE_FURTHER.** Investigate the supplier-sync envelope as one certified integration job; keep finite AI write sessions as an exploratory adjacent lead. No validated differentiated paid workflow exists yet. The useful shift is from pricing features to finite mutation authority, with explicit coverage and bypass boundaries.
